# Humanizer caught-tells ledger (mandatory check, cross-customer)

This is the workspace's own evidence base of AI tells CAUGHT IN OUR REAL DRAFTS, complementing the generic research catalog in `humanizer-research/`. The research catalog covers what the literature measured; this ledger covers what our production passes actually keep catching. Every humanizer run (any customer, any content type) must:

1. Read this ledger BEFORE Stage 1 and scan the draft against every entry.
2. Re-check against it during the Stage 5 critic pass.
3. Append any NEW tell caught in the same session, with date, source draft, example, and fix. The list only ever grows smarter.

Entries here are generic (voice-independent). Author-specific caps live in the author's voice profile (for Trophy/Charlie: `websites/trophy/learnings/charlie-voice-profile.md`, items 1-14, a hard rule). When both apply, the stricter rule wins.

No em-dashes anywhere. Dates are the day the tell was caught.

## The ledger

1. **Negation-contrast constructions. BANNED OUTRIGHT, not capped.** All forms: "It's not X. It's Y.", "Not X. Y.", "They're not X-ing. They Y.", the stamped "X, not Y" appositive, and headings shaped "Do X, Not Y". Originally capped at 2-3 per piece; the operator escalated to a full ban on 2026-07-17 after "They're not competing. They stop checking." and "Not a global table. Three people." survived FOUR passes under the cap-and-whitelist regime ("the most basic AI language, and it still keeps it"). Zero per piece, headings included. Plain factual negation inside a normal sentence stays legal ("missing a single opportunity did not materially affect the process"); what is banned is negation used as a rhetorical contrast or punch. Softer single-sentence "rather than" contrasts are tolerated at 1-2. Fix by restating the positive side ("ranked against just two other people" instead of "Not a global table. Three people."). (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness draft, operator escalation pass 5)
2. **Mic-drop aphorism ending every section.** Humans whiff some endings; a generator lands a quotable on 100% of sections. Cap strong closers at 2-3 and deliberately end at least two sections flat, on a link-out or a plain sentence. (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness draft)
3. **Too-neat abstract pivot sentences.** "The catch is architectural." Write the plain version ("There's a catch.") or fold the pivot into the next concrete sentence. (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness draft)
4. **Self-labeling honesty and labeled pivots with colons.** "The honest caveat: ..." / "The detail worth stealing: ...". State the content, drop the label. (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness draft, second instance caught in pass 4)
5. **Body/FAQ paraphrase-twins. The most persistent tell on record; recurred across three drafts and four passes.** The FAQ restates a body fact with only the adjectives changed, which reads as one generator sampling twice. Change the framing or the detail (derive a gap instead of repeating both numbers, keep an exact figure in only one place); an item list may live in the body or the FAQ, never both. (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness + personal-finance drafts)
6. **Perfect 3-4 part parallel builds.** Especially in verdict sections and TL;DRs. Break the grammar on at least one item or split into short/short/long. (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness draft)
7. **Comma-spliced appositive stacks.** A list crammed into a sentence with commas, usually an em-dash-avoidance artifact. Give the list its own sentence or a concrete parenthetical. (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness draft)
8. **Symmetric anaphora across adjacent sentences.** Mirrored echo ("pointed at X... Pointed at Y...") or a same-verb run ("Streaks mean... Leaderboards mean... Challenges mean... Wrapped means..."). Break the symmetry, change the verbs. (2026-07-17, Trophy personal-finance draft; verb-run variant caught in fitness pass 4)
9. **A second fragment run.** One hard fragment punch per piece can be authentic; a second triple-fragment anywhere turns the punch into a template. Fold the second into a normal sentence. (2026-07-17, Trophy personal-finance draft) UPDATE 2026-07-17: any fragment punch built on negation ("Not a global table. Three people.") falls under the entry-1 ban regardless of this allowance; the one-punch allowance survives only for non-negation fragments.
10. **Neat Q-then-A pairs.** Posing a tidy question and answering it in the next sentence. State the answer directly or let the question hang for a paragraph. (2026-07-17, Trophy personal-finance draft)
11. **Repeated link-in phrasing.** "covered in [link]" in adjacent sentences, or any one link-in formula 3+ times per piece. Vary the verbs or link the noun itself. (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness draft)
12. **Uniform long sentences in the TL;DR or intro.** Three or four 25-45 word sentences in a row read as one breath from a machine. Split one, keep one genuinely short. (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness draft)
13. **Mid-paragraph echo-stamp verdicts.** A two-or-three-word verdict that flips the previous sentence's noun back at the reader ("...doesn't explain it. Motivation does." / "...was never the workout. It was the scoreboard."). Survived three humanizer passes because each instance reads fine alone. At most one per piece, at a true argument peak; otherwise fold the verdict into the sentence. (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness draft, operator-flagged pass 4)
14. **Symmetric equation sentences.** An A-is-also-B mirror where both halves share the same unit ("every minute of progress is also a minute of engagement"). Machine elegance inside one sentence. Describe the concrete behavior instead. (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness draft, operator-flagged pass 4)
15. **Colons in body prose and FAQ answers.** Operator standing rule (see memory: minimize colons). Claim-then-payoff colons and recap colons both go; use two sentences, a parenthetical, or from/to. Structural labels (bold list leads, headings) are fine. Re-sync the schema FAQ after any FAQ edit. (2026-07-17, Trophy fitness draft, six caught in pass 4)

16. **A draft's own "AI-tells checklist walked, all clear" note does not mean it is clear. Re-run the critic anyway.** The Trophy consumer-apps pillar (2026-07-22) shipped a DRAFT QUALITY REPORT asserting "item 1 negation-contrast constructions at ZERO" and "item 14 no symmetric equation sentences". A fresh standalone humanizer pass then found three live negation-contrasts ("a product decision, not a technical default"; "The interesting part is not the list. It is that..."; "the streak engine is not the product. The lessons... are.") and a near-verbatim item-14 equation sentence ("every minute of practice is also a minute of engagement", the exact shape operator-flagged on the fitness draft). Lesson: a self-check done by the same pass that wrote the draft under-counts its own tells, especially items 1, 8, and 14. Never treat the in-draft checklist note as the humanizer run; the standalone critic pass is not optional. (2026-07-22, Trophy consumer-apps pillar)

17. **Within-piece favourite-idiom repetition.** One figurative phrase reused three times across non-adjacent paragraphs of a single piece: "at the center" 3x in the Trophy SaaS-API post ("a transaction sits at the center", "no checkout at the center", "whether a checkout sits at the center"); "earns its place / earn their keep" 3x in the social-apps post. Distinct from item 5 (body/FAQ twin) and item 8 (adjacent anaphora): this is the same short idiom scattered through one post, not two mirrored sentences and not an adjacent echo. A human notices they already used the image and reaches for a different one; a generator re-samples its highest-probability phrase for the same semantic slot. Keep the first use, restate the 2nd and 3rd concretely. Before saving, grep any distinctive two-or-three-word figurative phrase for 3+ hits in one piece. Caught only by a fresh adversarial pass AFTER the four drafts already cleared their per-draft humanizer runs, so it survives a per-post pass; the four passes each mapped their catches to existing items and missed it. (2026-07-24, Trophy 07-24 batch: SaaS + social-apps drafts)

18. **Batch-level sameness across sibling posts. Diff the whole skeleton and the shared strings, not just sentences.** A batch of posts written to one template converges on fleet-level tells that each post passes alone but that an adversarial reader clocks instantly side by side: the identical section skeleton in the identical order; the same "How Trophy Handles X" section opener ("Everything above can be built in-house, and the linked guides show..."); the same canned filler in the nav self-reference slot ("You're in the right place. Keep reading." in three of four posts); the same authority crutch ("well-documented" / "well understood" in three of four); and identical ordered capability lists (the seven SDKs in the same order in three of four). Ledger item 5 and the productivity recurrence note cover sentence-level sibling twins; this is the fleet-level cousin they do not cover. Before shipping a batch, diff each post's skeleton, its section openers, its nav block, and every enumerated list against its siblings, and force divergence on at least a couple (vary the Trophy-section opener, drop or reword the self-referential nav line, reorder the SDK list, cut the authority crutch to one instance across the batch). Fix applied 2026-07-24: nav self-references made post-specific, the twinned Trophy-section opener reworded, SDK lists put in three distinct orders, "well-documented" cut to one fleet instance, and the repeated idioms of item 17 restated. (2026-07-24, Trophy 07-24 batch: all four posts)

Independent agents writing the same source in different voices land on the same semantic move, and each one passes its own voice check.** The 2026-09-18 Beamcite X batch had four sub-agents draft one reply each per post in four measured registers (Avery, Alex, Arthur, Kshetez). On post 5 all four wrote "I am opening this" ("ok you got me, clicking" / "coming from the imgproxy guy i'm actually opening this" / "opening this blind off your reaction" / "okay opening this now"). On post 7 all four wrote the same single observation, that the same prompt returns different businesses on a rerun, two of them near-verbatim. On post 8 three of four opened on "i'm 17" plus "this is the only internet I have known". On post 2 there were two separate twin pairs inside one post (Avery and Kshetez both on "keep refreshing everything", Alex and Arthur both on "the ten minutes after it goes live are the worst"). This is NOT entry 18: entry 18 is one author writing siblings to one template across long posts. This is four authors, four different corpora, no shared template, and it still converges, because the source post constrains the highest-probability response so hard that the register differentiation collapses on top of one shared idea. It is invisible to each drafting agent by construction, since each one only ever sees its own register. Two mechanical signatures to grep: the same content word appearing in three or four of the sibling replies to one post, and identical character counts on the same clause shape (Avery's "the canvas never stood a chance" and Alex's "the storyboard is still a canvas" were both exactly 31 characters). Fix at the batch level, not the line level: for every source post, list the four replies side by side, and force at least two of them onto a DIFFERENT response function, not merely different wording. Changing "opening this" to "clicking" is not a fix; changing one register to ask about a mechanism and another to react to a specific detail is. Where the source admits only one honest response from this account (post 8, a stranger's political anger answered by a 17-year-old), the convergence is the evidence that the post should be skipped rather than replied to four ways.
